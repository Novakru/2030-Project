A dry 500-mL, three-necked round-bottomed flask equipped with a stirring bar, reflux condenser and rubber septa is purged with argon gas. 
Anhydrous magnesium dichloride (9.52 g, 100 mmol) and solid paraformaldehyde (4.50 g, 150 mmol) are added, 
while a slight positive pressure of argon is maintained. 
Dry tetrahydrofuran (250 mL) is added by syringe. Triethylamine (10.12 g, 100 mmol) is added dropwise by syringe and the mixture is stirred for 10 min. 
2-Bromophenol (8.65 g, 50 mmol) is added dropwise by syringe, resulting in an opaque, light pink mixture. 
This mixture is immersed in an oil bath at about 75°C (bath temperature) and soon turns a bright orange-yellow color. 
Heating at gentle reflux is maintained for 4 hr.
The reaction mixture is cooled to room temperature and 100 mL of ether is added. 
The resulting organic phase is transferred to a 1-L separatory funnel and washed successively with 
1 N HCl (3 × 100 mL) (Caution! gas evolves) and 
water (3 × 100 mL) (Note 10), 
dried over anhydrous magnesium sulfate (MgSO4), and filtered. 
The solvent is removed by rotatory evaporation leaving a pale yellow oil that solidifies on further vacuum drying at 1-2 mmHg. 
The resulting yellow solid (8.09-8.11 g, 80-81%) consisting mainly (=95%) of 3-bromosalicylaldehyde is sufficiently pure for further synthetic use.
